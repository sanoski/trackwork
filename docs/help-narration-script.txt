Welcome to the Vermont Rail System Maintenance of Way Tracker. If you spend your days putting in railroad ties, setting switches, and keeping the line in shape, this one was built for you. Think of it as command central for the crew's work. It keeps a running record of every tie, every switch, and every derail you install, and at the end of the week it turns all of that into a clean, professional report that lands in the office's inbox. No more clipboards flapping in the wind, and no more squinting at a coffee-stained spreadsheet. Just the numbers, the charts, and the credit your crew has earned. Let's take a tour.

The tracker organizes everything into two kinds of work. The first is a company line. That's ongoing railroad maintenance, like the Washington County Railroad's Connecticut River Division. A line can have worksites underneath it, such as Saint Johnsbury, so you can zoom in on one location or step back and see the whole line at once. The second kind is a sponsored job. That's a focused project with a tie goal and a deadline, like job nine forty-two. For those, the tracker keeps score against the goal, and even projects your finish date, so everyone knows exactly where things stand.

On the left, you'll find the sidebar. Think of it as your map of the work. Tap a line, and you'll see everything on it, all worksites combined. Tap a worksite beneath it, and you'll focus on just that site. Here's the part worth remembering: whatever you select in the sidebar is what the dashboard shows, and what your report will cover. Pick the whole line, and you get the whole line. Pick one worksite, and everything narrows to match. Simple.

Now, the part you'll use most: logging a day's work. Tap Log Work in the sidebar. One screen does it all. The date starts on today, but you can pick any date, so if the week got away from you, catching up on Tuesday's ties is no problem. Type in the ties installed for the day. Then, of those, how many were relay ties. Quick refresher. New ties are exactly that, freshly installed. Relay ties are reclaimed and reused, cleaned up and given a second life on the rail. The recycling program of the tie world. Relay is always part of the total, never on top of it. So if you put in two hundred ties and fifty of them were relays, you enter two hundred in the ties box and fifty in the relay box. The tracker figures out that the other one hundred fifty were new.

If the day was split across tracks, log it once per track with the same date. The second entry adds to the first, and the report shows the breakdown. On a company line, pick the worksite where the ties went in. A day belongs to one worksite. And if a machine went down, add a downtime row right there on the same screen: which machine, when it went down, and when it was back in service. The tracker works out the duration. Downtime can be submitted on its own, too, on a day with no ties at all. One thing you never type is the running total or the comparison to the daily target. The app recalculates those every time anything changes. That's the whole point.

Everyone makes mistakes, and the tracker expects it. On the dashboard, tap Edit on any row in the tie log or the downtime log. You can change the ties, the relay ties, one track's portion of a split day, or move the day to a different worksite. Everything downstream is recalculated. Downtime entries can be edited or deleted the same way. No calls to the office, no apologies, just fix it and move on.

Next, the Manage section. Start with switches and derails. Every switch or derail is its own named, dated record with a timber breakdown. For each timber length, you note how many were planned and how many went in, and whether that length is a head block. Tap Add Switch or Add Derail, fill in the name, the date, and the worksite, add a row for each length, and save. Derails come in three types: stationary, which has head block timbers; portable, which has no timbers at all; and switch, which is a switch to nowhere. On the dashboard and in the report, the diagrams show only timbers that were actually installed, while the tables keep the full planned versus actual record. Switch and derail timbers count toward Total Timbers Installed, but they're tracked separately from the daily tie count, so nobody ever confuses a switch timber with a tie.

Then worksites. These belong to company lines only. Add a worksite before you log work to it. Renaming one keeps all its history, because the work is tied to the site's ID, not its name. And a worksite that still has work logged against it can't be deleted, so nothing disappears by accident.

The rest of Manage is for administrators. Under Projects, you create a sponsored project or a company line, set its goal, deadline, and daily target, and change its status. Exactly one project is active at a time. Making one active moves the previous one to complete. Archived projects leave the sidebar but keep their data.

Under Users and Settings, you'll find four tabs. Users is where you add the crew. There are three roles. Admin does everything. Entry logs and corrects work. Viewer only reads. The last admin can never be removed, so you can't lock yourself out. Email and Names holds the organization name that prints on the report, the mail server, the From address, and who receives the weekly report. Send Test proves the mail settings work before you rely on them. Weekly Report is the timer. Turn it on, pick the day, the time, and the time zone, and the tracker sends the report once per week for the Sunday-through-Saturday week that just ended. It shows the next run, the last run, and any error. There's also a button to send this week's report right now. And the last tab is A I, or Claude. Here you can create a personal token so Claude can log work for you by voice or chat. Each token is tied to one account, expires on its own, and can be revoked at any time.

When you're ready for a report, look at the Report button at the top. It's smarter than it looks. It tells you what it's about to do, right there on the button. It might say Full line, or it might name a worksite like Saint Johnsbury, so you always know the scope before you tap. Give it a tap, and the report wizard opens. On the right side of the screen, or just below it on your phone, you'll see a live preview that updates the moment you change a setting. What you see is what the office gets.

Inside the wizard, the first choice is the worksite. You can build a report for the whole line, or for a single site. It starts on whatever you had selected in the sidebar, but you're free to change your mind right here, without backing out.

Next is the time period, and you've got four ways to slice it. Most recent reported week gives you the latest Sunday-through-Saturday week with work logged. That's your standard weekly report. Pick a week lets you grab any week you want; just choose a date inside it. Full project covers everything from the first day of work to the last, which is perfect for wrapping up a finished job, or showing off a full progress summary. And Date range lets you set a custom start and end. However you want to look at the work, the report won't judge.

Then comes deciding what goes in. Start with ties. You can include new and relay ties together, new only, relay only, or none at all. Next, switches and derails. Toggle these on to include your turnout and derail timber work on company lines. There's also a verbose option called Itemize switches and derails. Leave it off, and the report stays short and tidy: all your switches are summed into one neat timbers-by-length chart and a single table, and your derails do the same. Turn it on, and every single switch and derail gets its own diagram and its own table. That makes for a much longer report, so save it for when someone really needs every piece broken out. You can also include the charts: weekly production, cumulative totals, work composition, and a breakdown by worksite. The office folks do love a good chart. And finally, equipment downtime. One note there. Downtime is tracked at the line level, so if you've zoomed into a single worksite, it switches itself off automatically. One less thing to think about.

Once it looks right in the preview, sending it is easy. Type in the recipient's email address, and tap Generate and Send. The tracker renders everything to a polished PDF and emails it on the spot. And notice the email field always starts blank. That's on purpose, so you never accidentally fire a report off to the wrong person first thing in the morning. You decide who gets it, every time.

So what shows up in the finished report? That depends on the kind of work. A company line leads with the headline number, Total Timbers Installed, followed by a row of cards: switch timbers, switches, derails, new and relay ties, and days worked. After that come the charts, the switch and derail sections, the tie log, and the downtime table. A sponsored job leads with goal progress instead: how many ties remain, the percent complete, and a projected finish date, followed by your output against the target and the daily log. And remember those switches and derails? By default, the report consolidates them into one chart and one table, and simply lists which ones are included, instead of running on for pages. If you ever need the full breakdown, that's exactly what the Itemize toggle is for.

Two more things worth knowing. First, this screen isn't the only way in. Everything you can do here can also be done by talking to Claude, once an administrator connects it, or from the command line on the server. All three write the same data, so it doesn't matter which one you use. Second, down in the wizard there's an option called Rail installation projects, and it's grayed out. That's a placeholder for a new kind of work coming down the line. It doesn't do anything yet, so feel free to admire it, but don't bother tapping just yet.

And that's the tour. You log the work, the tracker does the math, and the office gets a sharp, professional report every week without you lifting a pen. Now go put in some ties, and let the numbers tell the story. Welcome aboard.
